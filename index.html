02 / The verdicts

Pick a lane.

Four routes through the coffee wilderness. None of them require a podcast.

01 / START HERE

Do you want café drinks?

If yes, accept that steaming milk is a skill and your first attempts will look like wet paint.

02 / THE HARDWARE

Milk on the menu?

Then invest in a dual boiler. A single boiler can do it, but so can a bicycle with one pedal.

03 / THE BEANS

Keep it fresh.

Buy a good grinder before an expensive machine. Espresso does not forgive optimism or stale beans.

Espresso verdict: Buy the grinder first. If milk drinks are non-negotiable, make it a dual boiler and stop pretending the thermoblock is a lifestyle choice.
01 / EASY WIN

Want clarity?

Choose a light to medium roast and let the cup taste like something other than charcoal.

02 / THE METHOD

Need reliable coffee?

Get a simple brewer, a scale, and a kettle. Three things. We are not building a spacecraft.

03 / THE RATIO

Start at 1:16.

One gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water. Adjust after tasting, not while panicking.

Filter verdict: The sensible gateway drug. It is inexpensive, delicious, and still leaves money for beans.
01 / BE HONEST

Do you enjoy precision?

The V60 rewards attention. It also remembers every time you poured too fast.

02 / THE POUR

Bloom, then pour.

Wet all the grounds first. Wait 30–45 seconds. Continue in slow, controlled circles.

03 / THE FIX

Sour or bitter?

Sour means more extraction. Bitter means less. Grind and pour accordingly. Yes, it really is that simple.

V60 verdict: Ideal for the curious person who thinks “one more variable” sounds like a relaxing Saturday.

Buy these.

  • A burr grinder with actual adjustment steps
  • A scale with a timer, because counting is not a personality
  • Fresh beans with a roast date
  • A kettle you can control, if you brew filter

Do not buy these yet.

  • A $2,000 machine with a $40 grinder
  • Every brewer that goes viral this month
  • Flavoured syrup pretending to be a tasting note
  • More storage jars than you have coffee
